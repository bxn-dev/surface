# Limitations

- A TCP timeout records only that the connection deadline elapsed; it does not prove firewall filtering.
- UDP is connectionless: a response proves reachability, an ICMP rejection can prove closure, but silence is reported as `open|filtered`. WireGuard intentionally ignores unauthenticated probes, so port 51820 can remain only a hint.
- Service identification uses safe banners, fixed read-only protocol discovery exchanges, TLS success, and port hints. Port assignments—including Wikipedia/IANA/vendor-derived names—are candidates, not proof; shared and customized ports remain ambiguous.
- CDN and reverse-proxy addresses may represent edge infrastructure rather than an origin server.
- HTTP security-header and cookie recommendations depend on application context. Header presence alone does not prove quality.
- Surface does not crawl, brute-force directories, authenticate, fuzz, exploit, deliver payloads, evade controls, or test denial of service.
- Surface does not provide complete vulnerability coverage or infer CVEs solely from banners.
- DNSSEC uses Hickory's local validator with built-in trust anchors and covers only selected primary-host A/AAAA RRsets. System or upstream resolver limitations, transport failures, and unsupported or inconclusive proofs are indeterminate; only cryptographic `Proof::Bogus` produces the high-severity finding.
- Dangling-CNAME indicators reuse only the first 16 ordered CNAME hops observed while resolving the primary target. Each destination receives selected A/AAAA lookups only: any address is `resolved`; uniformly authenticated NXDOMAIN is `nxdomain`; uniformly authenticated NOERROR/NODATA with SOA/NSEC/NSEC3 evidence is `no_address`; mixed, bogus, indeterminate, timed-out, or failed results are `indeterminate`. Only `nxdomain` produces a Medium potential indicator. Ownership, provider accounts, registration, claimability, and takeover feasibility are not tested; destination answers are never retained as addresses or propagated to port, service, HTTP, TLS, CT, or other scans.
- Wildcard DNS detection applies only to hostname targets and compares selected A/AAAA plus CNAME answers for exactly two random UUID-v4 children. Only identical non-empty answer sets are `detected`; authenticated empty answers are `not_detected`; mixed, rotating, incomplete, timed-out, or errored answers are `indeterminate`. Probe names and raw answers are never retained or used as scan targets. Detection is contextual routing evidence, not automatically a vulnerability.
- DKIM is queried only for explicitly supplied validated selectors; unqueried selectors and general DKIM availability remain unknown.
- MTA-STS/TLS-RPT/SPF/DMARC record presence does not prove secure mail delivery.
- SSH posture analysis reconnects once per deduplicated `SocketAddr` only for TCP services already identified as SSH. It accepts bounded SSH-2.0 or compatibility SSH-1.99 identification, sends one fixed Surface identification and one unencrypted KEXINIT, parses exactly one server KEXINIT packet, computes RFC 4253 client-first intersections including compression, then closes. Selections are inferred because Surface never performs key-exchange cryptography, requests host-key bytes, sends NEWKEYS, authenticates, opens an agent/channel/subsystem, or runs a command. Every pre-identification and identification line requires CRLF and rejects embedded controls. The identification is capped at 255 bytes including CRLF, after at most 50 pre-identification lines and 4 KiB including their CRLF bytes. The RFC total packet size is capped at 35,000 bytes, so its four-byte-excluding `packet_length` field is at most 34,996; each of ten name-lists is capped at 8 KiB and 128 names, and each name is at most 64 printable ASCII bytes excluding commas with at most one validated `@domain` suffix. Terminal and HTML presentation caps every imported SSH detail key/value and banner at 255 Unicode scalar values, removes terminal controls, and HTML-escapes the result. Malformed, incompatible, no-common, timed-out, cancelled, or otherwise indeterminate exchanges never produce an SSH posture finding or expand scan scope. Only `complete_inferred` and an exact selected `diffie-hellman-group14-sha1`, `diffie-hellman-group1-sha1`, `ssh-rsa`, `ssh-dss`, `3des-cbc`, `arcfour`, `arcfour128`, `arcfour256`, `hmac-sha1`, `hmac-md5`, or `hmac-md5-96` value produces the Medium/medium-confidence `NET-SSH-LEGACY-ALGORITHM`. Mere server advertisement and software banners do not; the result is inferred before completed key exchange and does not establish server preference or a CVE.
- TLS analysis makes one normal validating handshake per deduplicated applicable implicit-TLS endpoint; it does not try protocol negotiation such as STARTTLS, brute-force alternate protocol/cipher combinations, disable validation, or reconnect for certificate-transparency recovery. A private per-connection recorder retains bounded presented-certificate evidence before returning the normal WebPKI verifier's result unchanged. Rejection remains a failed handshake; trust and hostname status remain unknown without independent typed evidence. Surface inspects at most 16 peer certificates and 1 MiB cumulative DER, retains at most 128 unique normalized DNS/IP SANs, and marks SAN truncation or other evidence limits. DER is dropped after parsing and never retained in reports or logs. Public-key bits remain unknown unless x509-parser reliably reports them. Medium/high-confidence weakness findings require exact direct fields: `rsaEncryption` OID `1.2.840.113549.1.1.1` with nonzero `public_key_bits < 2048`; one of RFC 3279's SHA-1 signature OIDs `1.2.840.113549.1.1.5`, `1.2.840.10040.4.3`, or `1.2.840.10045.4.1`; or exact `protocol_version` `TLSv1_0`/`TLSv1_1`. Current rustls handshakes offer TLS 1.2/1.3, so obsolete-version interpretation primarily preserves exact imported observations and is not exhaustive protocol testing. Surface emits no broad TLS cipher weakness finding because the normal handshake does not directly exercise known insecure suites.
- HTTPS/header analysis uses a native HTTP client and does not execute JavaScript.
- The exposure score reflects only generated findings. Missing observations never deduct points; compare scores only when coverage and score-model versions are compatible.
- SARIF and CycloneDX are lossy integration projections; use Surface JSON for complete observations and errors.
- Offline CVE matches require exact recognized product/version evidence and remain candidates, not confirmed exploitable vulnerabilities. Bundle freshness and completeness are operator responsibilities.
- DNS queries cover the primary target and standard mail-policy names; Surface does not enumerate every possible subdomain.
- AXFR is attempted only when primary-host SOA evidence and exact-owner NS records establish one unambiguous zone; Surface never guesses a parent zone. At most 4 NS names, 2 addresses per NS, and 8 deduplicated TCP endpoints are considered. Each endpoint is bounded to 2 MiB, 4,096 answer records, 64 messages, the request timeout, cancellation, and the caller's absolute deadline. Only a complete matching authoritative opening/closing SOA transfer is `allowed`; transferred owner names and records are never retained or scanned.
- CNAME addresses returned while resolving the primary target are scanned, but CNAMEs do not trigger unrelated-host discovery.
- Certificate Transparency results contain certificate names, not proof that a host currently exists or belongs to the target owner. CertSpotter queries are limited to three 1 MiB pages and 1,000 in-scope names; discovered names are never scanned automatically.
- Reverse-NS results can be stale or incomplete. An exact nameserver-pair match indicates shared DNS infrastructure, not common ownership; only the first 300 results per nameserver are considered.
- Passive intelligence sends the target hostname to the configured third-party API provider.
- A clean report or score of 100 does not prove that a target is secure.
